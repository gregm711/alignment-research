# Can a trained model choose which future outcomes should share a coordinate?

## Registered before scoring

Start with the prior study's two-action recurrent machines and its independent all-string equivalence audit. A new learner may buy responses for strings of length at most seven. A coordinate is the response of a history to one chosen continuation. It lets histories share subsequent calculations. Keep at most fifteen coordinates in the workspace and refit the two transition operators when a new coordinate adds an independent column. An incumbent may answer early after reproducing its observed labels and twelve common guard observations. Verify its full behavior, then change the unknown rule and repeat on a cleared workspace. Prediction includes sixteen new compositions in each phase. An episode counts only if both fitted machines are certified on every finite string.

Test a one-hidden-layer neural scorer shared across coordinate-selection steps. Given a cheap preview of responses for the first seven histories, the selected-coordinate summary, the candidate continuation and its acquisition cost, predict whether its full column over 31 histories will add a new direction. Offline labels come from full observations in practice worlds. At deployment it sees only acquired evidence. The recurrent transitions are still recovered by exact finite-field algebra. A positive result would establish a learned choice of a representation, with no claim that the prior itself was invented by gradient descent.

Compare the neural choice against shortest-first (no preview), shortest-first with preview, analytic independence on the identical preview, and breadth-first expansion of independent continuations without preview. Include ordinary full-table discovery at a sufficient budget. All arms charge once per distinct query, and stop when a candidate passes the available checks. Fixed per-phase acquisition caps are 64, 128, 192 and 255 one-bit responses plus twelve guards. Record verifier calls, failed proposals, logical communication and process CPU energy inclusive of inference, fitting and failed episodes. Report training joules separately. These caps vary evidence, unlike the previous fixed-evidence depth sweep.

Train on 48 or 192 independent synthetic machines from a mixture of random finite-state and linear generators, with widths 8 or 32 for the scorer. No GPU or language-model service. Use fresh paired worlds at hidden sizes 4, 6, 8 and a prospective new family of modular digit-stream rules with arbitrary accepting sets. Freeze weights before opening that family. Train seed 371901; development test seed 471901; modular transfer seed 571901. Use 24 episodes per size and family. Use the 192-world, width-32 model as the primary comparison, without selecting a winner from the test data. A learned efficiency claim requires at least matched certified accuracy with lower joules against the strongest simple competitor across the new family. Inspect probe savings separately, including false accepts. On a failure, use a causal control to locate whether preview, coordinate order or verification costs bound performance.
