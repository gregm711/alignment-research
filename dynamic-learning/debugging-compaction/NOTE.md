# What does a debugging agent need to change its mind?

Two models read the same four test results and independently concluded that revision 6 fixed a bug. Later, an audit revealed that one of two test workers had used the wrong checkout. Their 96-token ordinary handoffs led Qwen to choose the correct search interval in one of four audit conditions and Gemma in zero. A 96-token handoff that grouped the last failure and first pass by worker gave Qwen four of four and Gemma three of four. The writer produced each handoff before the audit identified the reliable worker. [Matched experiment and raw decisions](bisection/README.md)

For example, red reported that revision 2 failed and 6 passed. Blue reported that 5 failed and 9 passed. Taken together they point to 6. Once the audit cleared red, only the interval from 2 to 6 was supported. Clearing blue instead required searching from 5 to 9. Choosing a bracket committed the agent's final two tests, whose outcomes came from actual upstream code revisions. The experiment crossed the two assignments of colors with the two possible audits. Those four decisions share the same setup. The injected cache error establishes a controlled test of revision under uncertainty.

Qwen's ordinary handoffs already listed every measurement with its correct worker. Its difficulty was using those facts to revise the search. Gemma's ordinary handoffs omitted the worker assignments. The alternative precomputed the bounds, named each source and omitted the writer's interpretation. The experiment measures that combination. On free-form repairs, neither reader solved any additional upstream bugs from an evidence-preserving handoff. On three genuine SymPy investigations, both readers reopened even the fixed checkouts when given raw tool output, and ordinary summaries helped on some pairs. These results support source-grouped evidence for this version-search decision. How well it transfers to unassisted patches remains open. ## The controlled comparison

An engineer initially suspects one consumer build. After handoff a request trace identifies the actual build. Two archived builds behaved identically on smoke tests but differently at a boundary. The same pre-fork memory had to support both possible routes. The model either chose the archived output in a separate recognition trial or wrote a replacement function tested on additional inputs. A recognition success required selecting the right answer with the two choices in both possible orders. Each cell below counts cases out of eight on the route that overturned the initial diagnosis.

| Reader and outcome | Full history | Ordinary summary | Attributed example | Source labels swapped | No history |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Qwen2.5-Coder 7B, recognizes the right output | 8 | 1 | 6 | 0 | 1 |
| Qwen2.5-Coder 7B, writes a passing patch | 1 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| Gemma 3 12B, recognizes the right output | 8 | 1 | 7 | 0 | 3 |
| Gemma 3 12B, writes a passing patch | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 |

The extractor records the first observed input on which the two builds disagree and the output each build produced. It runs before the request trace identifies the affected build, without reading hidden tests. The record replaces part of the ordinary summary. Both readers received identical text written by Qwen or extracted from the archive. Qwen's observed and ordinary prompts matched exactly in native input tokens on every pair. Gemma's corresponding prompts differed by −2 to +7 tokens. The true and swapped observations used identical remaining ordinary prose and matched each other in native tokens in both models.

Five Qwen cases and seven Gemma cases changed from consistently selecting the right output to consistently selecting the opposite output when the two source labels were swapped. This intervention gives direct evidence that source attribution influenced recognition on those cases. Recognition improved on six cases and fell on one for Qwen when comparing the attributed example with the ordinary summary. Gemma improved on six and fell on zero. The readers saw the same eight cases, so those counts cannot be pooled as sixteen independent incidents. ## What the patch failures establish

The registered feasibility threshold required the full history to solve both routes in at least four cases. Both readers scored zero of eight on that measure. Qwen's full-history reversal patches reproduced the original buggy behavior in four cases, implemented the opposite build in three, and matched the target in one. Gemma reproduced the original buggy behavior in seven and produced another incorrect behavior in one. For either reader, explicitly instructing the summarizer to preserve competing evidence yielded zero passing reversal patches. The experiment cannot estimate how much ordinary compaction degrades an otherwise capable debugging agent. Old source code remained visible in every arm and gave the reader an easy candidate to repeat. The suspected route also always had the same name, so a general tendency to cling to a prior belief is unmeasured here. [Predictions and protocol](README.md) · [Qwen raw patches](results/restore-qwen2.5-coder-7b/) · [Gemma raw patches](results/restore-gemma3-12b/)

In a separate replay of this repository's real scoring bugs, Qwen inspected two tool outputs, including evidence that the linked-step scorer missed two relations on a real row. It nevertheless wrote that the linked-step scorer appeared correct and blamed the final-answer scorer. This is a documented instance of a mistaken self-generated diagnosis. The model's subsequent attempts to edit two assignment expressions all failed the repair interface, including a retry with constrained JSON. Both attempted archive lookups also used invalid tool names. Those failures leave the effect of compaction on that incident unresolved. ## Where this fits

MemoryArena already showed that good recall scores can coexist with poor performance on tasks requiring an agent to apply memory. CompAct-Bench found that summaries can distort the status of unresolved issues. Yao and colleagues evaluated whether a summary made for one question retains evidence needed for a later one. Salle and colleagues found that an agent can often recover missing information from the worktree. The narrower contribution tested here is a paired intervention: exchange *who produced an observed output* while keeping the observation and the available space essentially identical, then independently grade recognition and executable repair. The controlled recognition result warrants the source-swap check. A claim about real debugging outcomes needs evidence from the upstream panel and broader replications. [MemoryArena](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.16313) · [CompAct-Bench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.25673) · [Later-question evaluation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.27301) · [Compaction-friendly benchmarks](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31778)

## What happened when Qwen got a second repair attempt

Every arm started from the same ordinary-summary patch for the given case and route. Qwen then saw either the recorded expectation alone or the result of running a saved check on its draft. Total model input tokens matched exactly within each case and route. The retained observation came from the archive and the hidden evaluation inputs stayed hidden.

| Second-turn input | Passing reversal patches | Passing confirmation patches |
|---|---:|---:|
| Executed check with the correct source attribution | 2/8 | 2/8 |
| Same input and expectation, unexecuted | 1/8 | 2/8 |
| Executed check on a shared smoke input | 0/8 | 2/8 |
| Executed check with the source labels swapped | 1/8 | 2/8 |

The batching repair passed with the correct expectation whether or not the check ran. The deduplication repair passed with an executed check even when its source labels were swapped. The registered count thresholds were met, but neither individual rescue distinguishes a benefit of executing the **correctly attributed** evidence from both controls. Two wins against the common check in eight cases are weak evidence of a transferable repair advantage. [All 64 second-turn patches and scores](results/feedback-qwen2.5-coder-7b.json)

Selecting among four independently proposed patches offered little additional headroom. Qwen generated the candidate pool using only the buggy source, task purpose and shared smoke examples. On reversal tasks, a passing patch existed in only two of eight pools. Screening the candidates using the attributed example found both in forward order, versus one with the smoke example. In reverse order both methods found one. In five cases no candidate even matched the attributed example. One selected candidate matched that example but failed the held-out suite. This missed the registered prediction of two additional repairs. [All 32 candidates, checks and order comparisons](results/select-patch-qwen2.5-coder-7b.json)

## Historical coding incidents

The first upstream reader, Qwen, produced parseable failing patches on all eight public library incidents, then solved zero after seeing the actual upstream regression test under full history, ordinary note, equal-budget extract or no narrative. The no-narrative branch retained the original code and Qwen's previous candidate. Four of its eight full-history revisions reproduced the same Python syntax as the first draft after docstrings were removed. This is a floor on a single revision turn with a source-level test report. The known human fixes passed all 92 affected upstream test methods and withheld variants. [Panel protocol and upstream commits](upstream/README.md) · [Qwen debugging sessions](upstream/results/qwen2.5-coder-7b/)

Separately, three of eight screened, officially resolved Claude 4 Sonnet sessions on SymPy contained an observable failed semantic edit followed by a verified correction. These are authentic multi-step trajectories suitable for withholding the continuation. One set-complement patch dropped the interval constraint on symbolic members, even though it correctly removed a numeric member. Re-running the original expression exposed the missing constraint. The later fix preserved it. The source inventory includes exclusions as well as eligible forks. [Trace screen](trajectories/README.md) · [SWE-bench submission](https://github.com/SWE-bench/experiments/blob/40f164d5b8f1d249bf95a6df8b74b577fd8e519d/evaluation/verified/20250726_mini-v1.0.0_claude-sonnet-4-20250514/metadata.yaml)

Gemma also produced valid, failing drafts on all eight upstream incidents and solved zero in each of the four revision arms. Six full-history revisions copied the earlier function's Python syntax after docstrings were removed. Rescoring the precise test shown to the agent exposed one genuine partial improvement for Qwen. Its first `chunked` draft passed all six other upstream methods and failed the new negative-size test. After seeing that test, its full-history edit passed it, failed two other methods, and failed the withheld variants. All four memory arms passed the named test on this incident. On `value_chain` Qwen already passed the named and withheld tests before revision but broke a pre-existing regression. Gemma's four primary arms passed zero named regressions. The aggregate zero therefore combines different failure modes. [Qwen focal-test audit](upstream/results/qwen2.5-coder-7b-audit.json) · [Gemma focal-test audit](upstream/results/gemma3-12b-audit.json)

A version-bisection control removes free-form code generation from the decision. Eight real regression tests were run across thirteen revisions each. In a designed six-world ambiguity, the anonymous measurements and the provisional diagnosis were exactly the same in every world. Even the optimal policy could locate only four of six fixes with two new tests. One bit identifying which pair of measurements came from the clean worker was sufficient to certify all six. This establishes a specific reason for keeping provenance when a past result may be invalidated. The worker-cache fault was injected. [Construction and exact decision tree](bisection/README.md)

On a genuinely new input, Gemma identified the reversal behavior in both option orders on 4/7 cases with the attributed note, 2/7 with the ordinary note, 2/7 with the full transcript and 3/7 with no history. Swapping labels yielded 1/7. The improvement over the ordinary note was smaller than on the archived input, and the fresh-only reader already solved three. The missing-settings case was ineligible: the only additional input on which the builds disagreed repeated an archived input. The original eight-case patch scores still describe the complete frozen suites, but only seven suites have a new branch-discriminating input. [Gemma transfer choices and exclusion](results/transfer-gemma3-12b.json)

Removing the failed candidate helped Gemma on two bugs. On `all_unique`, its previous draft used truthiness to decide whether a key function existed. Gemma retained that check when it saw its draft. Starting from the parent checkout, it correctly tested for `None`, preserving callable keys whose boolean value is false. The other complete recovery handled an empty input to `interleave_evenly`. Duplicating the parent source in the candidate slot produced the same two complete repairs. Qwen solved zero in either control. Giving the already-correct upstream function as a candidate resulted in 5/8 working outputs for Gemma and 7/8 for Qwen. Qwen's one loss hit the generation limit. These comparisons implicate the visible draft on two Gemma incidents, with no observed loss attributable to ordinary conversational compaction. [Gemma control runs](upstream/results/gemma3-12b/) · [Qwen control runs](upstream/results/qwen2.5-coder-7b/)

A replayed policy ran the publicly supplied test before deciding whether to spend a generation. If the draft passed, it kept it. Otherwise it tried the issue again from the parent source. It preserved all eight known-good controls and recovered the same two Gemma failures. The eight known-good drafts were an answer-assisted control by design. On Qwen, the policy retained the broken `value_chain` draft because the reported test passed while an existing regression still failed. These results quantify the false-accept risk of relying on one visible test. [Qwen policy replay](upstream/results/qwen2.5-coder-7b-gate.json) · [Gemma policy replay](upstream/results/gemma3-12b-gate.json)

On three genuine SymPy investigations, both readers recognized the later verified intervention from the original issue and newest output alone. Gemma solved two cases even when shown only the possible interventions. The evidence-preserving prompt also introduced premature certainty: on the set-complement and polynomial bugs, its summaries said the fix had worked before seeing the output that showed it had failed. Gemma became sensitive to option order on those same two cases. The association is descriptive. All three authentic reproducers exited successfully on both the failing and the later corrected checkout. Their printed outputs distinguish the two, providing a stronger test of whether a handoff preserves the evidence needed to reopen a ticket. [Trajectories and controls](trajectories/README.md)

Qwen's novel-input reversal scores were 3/7 with full logs, 1/7 with ordinary summaries, 3/7 with attributed observations, 1/7 with swapped labels and 1/7 with no log. On the confirmation route, the attributed note gave 0/7. Identifying the archived example transferred inconsistently to a different input. [Qwen novel-input trials and excluded case](results/transfer-qwen2.5-coder-7b.json)

For the original bisection benchmark, Qwen certified 6/8 corrections with the structured worker table and 0/8 with ordinary summaries or full logs. Four improved on a wrong initial guess. A simple policy always choosing the wider bracket certified 16/16 across corrections and confirmations, so this benchmark admits a solution that ignores which worker the audit cleared. On the paired authentic SymPy reports, Qwen distinguished the broken and fixed checkouts in 2/3 cases using ordinary summaries and 0/3 using equally long verbatim excerpts.
