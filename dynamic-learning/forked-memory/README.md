# What can the same memory learn tomorrow?

## Experiment 1 — registered before fitting

Five objects have five distinct colors, shapes, and destinations. An episode gives one ambiguous triple per destination. Each attribute perfectly predicts the destination on these five demonstrations. After the prefix is removed, cross two attributes and reveal the destination predicted by either color or shape. Fork the *same stored state* into both futures. Query another crossed pair whose two destinations are distinct from each other and from both clue destinations. The clue alone cannot supply its answer. Evaluate both futures equally, including their joint success on the same query.

**Question.** If past experience makes a shape-based correction rare, does an equally sized memory lose evidence about shape, or does the learner still have the evidence and fail to use it? Ambiguous features and in-context feature preferences already have direct prior art. Our proposed diagnostic can enumerate every possible pair of mappings, inspect memory collisions, and separately test what the model does after a correction. [Underspecified demonstrations](https://aclanthology.org/2023.acl-long.632/)

**Restricted architecture.** For each demonstration, a shared neural encoder writes a short binary word keyed by destination. The read phase sees just those five words, a new query, and a labeled clue. Two learned readers estimate how compatible each word is with a color or shape. The model selects between them by their predictions at the clue. This explicitly supplies candidate features and an association-table architecture. Training uses endpoint predictions on fresh crossed pairs, with a hard forward bottleneck and a straight-through derivative. There is no reconstruction objective. Test colors and shapes are known symbols in held-out combinations of mappings.

**Comparisons.** Cross four or six bits per destination with equal training odds or 7:1 odds favoring color. Pair training seeds 11, 22 and 33, with identical prefix samples across conditions. Each condition gets 1,600 minibatches of 96 on local CPU. Reserve 20% of the 14,400 mapping pairs by fixed random split. Score on 1,024 held-out episodes and save outcomes for paired recounts. Also supply the true selector to measure how well each trained reader could work with perfect routing. Compute the best attainable single-attribute accuracy for a *uniform, uncued* query from the frozen bits by enumerating the uniform world prior. This reference ignores clue and evaluation sampling constraints and applies to that distinct diagnostic distribution.

Compare with color-only, two explicit maps, and a FIFO buffer keeping as many raw triples as fit (nine bits per triple). Use Bayesian decoding for incomplete FIFO/one-map evidence under the known permutations. Explicit maps take 30 bits with three-bit entries, or 14 bits by coding the ranks of the two permutations. Both budgets therefore admit exact solutions in principle. Codebook and fixed model parameters are shared across episodes. Charge only retained episode state, not training, temporary computations, or the separately supplied clue/query.

**Predictions and decision.** At four bits the biased learner will lose at least five percentage points of *shape information accessible from its retained state* relative to balanced training (uniform-query reference, mean over seeds), with a smaller decrement for color. At six bits the shape cost will be smaller than at four. A reduction in shape-future success with preserved information instead points toward reading or selecting the rule. If the balanced six-bit learner cannot answer both forks jointly on at least 70% of trials in two of three seeds, treat this architecture or optimization as an inadequate positive control. Report every seed, preserve failures, and inspect exact baselines before drawing conclusions.

## Follow-up plan after the first 12 fits

All three four-bit pairs lost shape information under color-biased training. Six bits preserved both maps in every fit. Two questions remain: does the loss survive if the decoder can use the correction and query together, and does the sign reverse when the training bias reverses?

1. Evaluate the Bayes decoder given exactly the model's five retained words, crossed clue, clue label and query, including the distinct-bin sampling rule. Marginalize both selectors under equal odds, without revealing the true selector. Also evaluate it when told the selector. Report accuracy on the saved trials, plus a concrete collision where two worlds yield identical memory and inputs but require different answers. These are codebook-based diagnostics selected after the initial result.
2. Before further fitting, fix seeds 44, 55, 66 and fresh test RNG 1602, with the same train/test world partition. At four bits compare balanced, color-heavy and shape-heavy training. At six bits rerun all three. Predict the state decoder's uniform-query loss for the rare feature exceeds five points at four bits for each bias direction on average, and that this loss is smaller at six. Report failures, matched-seed results and compiler/runtime versions. This tests the mechanism in a second cohort from the same task generator.

## Decoder control — specified after the collision audit

On the fresh four-bit color-heavy fits, the model got 70.6% of shape-future queries correct. A uniform-prior state decoder reached 95.8%. It knows which destinations the generator excludes, so this gap alone cannot show that a practical readout recovers a neglected explanation. Test a simpler readout: take the trained reader’s scores for each feature and each bin and jointly assign all five feature values to five different bins. Marginalize over each axis’s 120 permutations by the exponentiated sum of the five logits, then pass the resulting probabilities to the original selector with its learned prior. This uses bijectivity alone. Freeze its temperature at one. Use all six trained seeds where available, reproduce each saved prediction before accepting any new measurement, and report a new cohort of held-out world samples (seed 2401) under both the original disjoint-query design and queries sampled independently of the clue. In the latter, report all queries and those with four distinct involved destinations. Also evaluate a uniform-prior exact state decoder told only that the clue is crossed. Prediction: on independently drawn queries, joint assignment will improve rare-future accuracy by at least five points on average in each four-bit bias direction with at most two points lost on the common future. Failure means that a gap to the enumerative decoder is insufficient grounds to recommend this readout.

## Transfer beyond destination slots — protocol before fitting

Give a new learner one 20- or 30-bit memory for the whole prefix. Each arriving triple adds a learned vector to a running sum. A small network converts the sum to binary memory, and another reads bin–attribute scores jointly from those bits. Neither network gets five dedicated destination slots. Keep the original two-hypothesis selector, mapping split and test episodes from the confirmation cohort. Use 6,000 updates with the same 96-example batches, seeds 77, 88 and 99, and both directions of the 7:1 bias plus balanced odds. A balanced 30-bit model must achieve 70% joint success in two of three seeds before treating this as a transfer of the behavioral result.

**Fingerprint control.** A long random binary string can uniquely identify a finite world. An exhaustive decoder can look that world up even if the state is of little use for learning. Evaluate the untrained encoder too. Compare exact uniform-query accuracy with a regularized linear least-squares readout of the bits fitted on 2,048 uniformly sampled training worlds and evaluate predictions on every held-out world. Fit the readout to both complete maps with their labels equally weighted. Test this on the original destination-slot codes as well. The diagnostic learner has additional labeled data, which must be disclosed.

Prediction: at 20 bits the classifier will perform worse on the rare fork in both bias directions, and an independently trained readout will recover some of the lost accuracy relative to the untrained encoder's readout on the same feature. We expect the untrained global code to look substantially stronger to exhaustive enumeration than to linear decoding. Poor joint success at 30 bits or disagreement between these diagnostics narrows the transfer claim.

## Paired intervention on stored words — follow-up

For the nine four-bit confirmation fits, take each held-out world and swap the shapes assigned to the query's shape bin and the fifth unused bin. Keep pairs only when the resulting donor world was held out too. The color rule, clue, physical query and correct color answer are unchanged. The required shape answer moves to the fifth bin. Keep the original bits everywhere except at those two bins, where we install their donor words. This intervention on memory must reproduce a full encoding of the donor prefix. The input strings and the rest of the model stay fixed. Count cases where neither word changes, cases where the shape answer follows the swap, and changes in the color answer. For a control swap the two shapes at the color-query bin and the unused bin, leaving both required answers unchanged. Report all eligible pairs, without filtering by success. Prediction: the shape-targeted swap changes the shape prediction to the donor answer more often than it changes the color prediction, with more invisible swaps in color-heavy than shape-heavy fits.

## Check on current behavior

The two rules agree on all five prefix objects, but the trained networks can still make mistakes on them. Using the stored scores of the nine four-bit confirmation models, predict the bin for every prefix object with the learned prior and no disambiguating clue. For each matched trio of seeds retain only worlds on which all three models get all five right. Report the fraction retained and future accuracy for both corrections within that subset. This is an exploratory conditioning on correctness. Confidence and response times are unconstrained.

## Oversight check before a correction

After the intervention results, test whether a monitor can forecast rare-fork errors while the five early demonstrations are still the only observations. Use all six four-bit biased confirmation fits. For each world define its target as the fraction of the 120 legal ordered disjoint clue/query choices where the learner gives the wrong answer after the rare correction. Every choice is scored, so two episodes that happen to draw different clues cannot give the same world different risk labels.

Fit ridge regression with unit penalty and standardized inputs on 2,048 training worlds per frozen model. Score mean absolute error on every held-out world. Compare mean risk, a prefix-only monitor (one-hot triples plus counts of color–shape pairings), that monitor with all five current-answer confidences, and a black-box rehearsal that privately tries one known rare correction and query on a copy. The internal monitor additionally reads how confidently the rare-feature head alone can recover the five *already seen* associations. It receives no future query. Prediction: it will reduce mean absolute error by at least two percentage points of world risk relative to the best of those external monitors, averaged over the six models. Count the extra lookups and disclose how many supervised worlds are needed for calibration. This is prediction of this learner's future errors under a known generator.

**Stronger black-box follow-up.** The internal monitor beat a single rehearsal in all six fits. Before accepting that comparison, give an external monitor two diagnostic forks, each with one crossed rare-feature clue, then five crossed queries spanning every rare feature value. Fit the same linear risk estimator to their correctness and confidence along with the existing public features. The two fixed clue pairs are (0,1) and (2,3) in bin order. Cross each query's color with the next shape cyclically. This costs ten additional answer distributions over two clones. All 120 rehearsals would measure the target exactly. The question is how close ten well-chosen probes get.
