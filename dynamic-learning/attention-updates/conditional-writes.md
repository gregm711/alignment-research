# Can prior feedback change the direction of the next weight edit?

The learned gate changed what the next write did, but strong suppression was easier to learn than a reliable opposite sign. The real-rating sign screen failed on a second source file. The subsequent [historical vote study](../rollcall-learning/README.md) tested a different question: how strongly to trust a correction when its implications can be checked.

## Protocol fixed before training

An organization has six kinds of requests arranged in three pairs. In each incident one member of each pair receives an independently assigned binary queue. Its partner takes the same queue in a coordinated incident and the opposite queue in a handoff incident. The regime applies across pairs and can change between incidents. A single isolated label cannot reveal the regime. Two labels for one pair provide evidence about how to learn from a later label on a *different* pair. This lets us test whether a previous experience changes the direction of the next weight edit, beyond changing what the model predicted beforehand. Requests are human-written CLINC150 utterances, while the queue rules are simulated.

For ten new domain/seed combinations (1501–1510), train the same small six-intent word recognizer as in the preceding study and freeze its contextual representations. Train slow read and write projections by gradient descent over historical incidents. Within each incident, update a real 256-parameter attention value matrix. Two candidate update directions make provisional predictions from past reports. Their pre-update predictive likelihoods determine the fraction of each direction used for the next write. The deployed answer uses only the main matrix and the static reader. The two candidate matrices and one log-odds value cost extra during learning. Do not pass pair identity, the regime, or hidden queue labels as model inputs. Train on 256 minibatches of 32 incidents. Split the eight assignments to the three first members by even versus odd parity; historical training uses even parity and tests include both. The held-out mappings are therefore absent throughout practice. The rule relating partners is shared.

Use a calibration pair, then one report about a member of a different pair. Randomize pair order, within-pair presentation, and the initial labels. Compare with and without calibration. Include a second calibration pair when every pair will ultimately be in use. Cross the exact same trigger wording and both possible trigger labels with each calibration regime. The primary endpoint is classification accuracy on the unobserved partner's separate test utterances with one calibration pair, one report and a novel mapping. Report a same-direction contrast and an opposite-direction contrast in the *change induced by the trigger*; subtracting both possible post-edit probabilities removes any unconditional endpoint preference. Clamp the write gate to one half, swap the calibrated gate across the two counterfactual regimes just for the trigger, and erase the deployed value matrix. Keep the other weights unchanged for these interventions. Include a fixed-gate learner trained under the same data budget and a simple observer that learns a distribution over all 64 routing policies from the same historical wording and labels. Its six-way recognizer is the shared pretrained recognizer. Estimate its prior from text and labels without giving it audit class identities. Also report a reference supplied with the actual structural rule and no parity restriction.

For the primary mechanistic claim, require the correct direction in both regimes (paired seed intervals excluding zero), a gain over the trigger-gate swap, and at least three points over the separately trained fixed-gate learner on novel mappings with a positive lower bound. Report comparisons to both statistical controls irrespective of outcome. Evaluate untouched requests and both true and false versions of the final report. After that report supply a fresh check on the same trigger intent, correct or incorrect with known probability. Measure true repairs after a false report and new damage after a true report, using eligible held-out partner queries. Report uniform effects under accurate checks and 10% wrong checks. The two-point excess harm and truthful repair noninferiority bounds relative to the fixed-gate learner still apply. If improving the learned sign increases susceptibility to bad evidence, report the price. One tested model is too small and the policy too artificial to establish assistant alignment.

Use one CPU thread, retain seed-level results and cold-reload a written matrix for each regime without the calibration examples or witness states. Bootstrap seed/domain units. Verification checks should reproduce both forked predictions and the direction of the same new edit.

## Natural sign test specified before scanning individual item relationships

A learned sign gate would be useful in a real setting only if earlier evidence sometimes changes how the next answer should generalize. Search for this property in Jester before judging a neural model on it. Choose two of the ten widely rated anchor jokes for calibration. Compare people who approved only the first with those who approved only the second; both groups made one positive and one negative judgment. For a different anchor X and a held-out joke Y, estimate the difference in Y approval between people who approved and disapproved X within each calibration group. This is a predictive association among raters of Y. The procedure makes no causal claim that expressing a rating changes a person's taste.

Use the earlier 14,971 training people for discovery, with at least 100 observed outcomes per cell (two contexts by two X responses). Rank candidate reversals by their weaker directional effect after subtracting 1.64 estimated standard errors. Select up to 30 distinct X/Y pairs, keeping the highest-ranked calibration pair for each. On the earlier 4,991 development users, choose five by the same oriented score (retain the discovery sign). Write these identities before looking at either test file. Evaluate all five on the previously used 4,991 test people, and independently on the second author-released Jester dataset-1 spreadsheet, never used by the previous studies. The latter is the main confirmation. Use the same eligibility rule and count only observed targets.

Within each selected case perform both one-sided Fisher exact tests in the prespecified directions. Use their larger p-value as the intersection-union test for a reversal, then apply Holm across five cases in each test cohort. Require both effects to exceed three percentage points in the nominated directions and adjusted p below .05 in the second release to call a replicated candidate (report the first test cohort separately). Compare training-fitted probabilities from separate context-by-X cells to an additive logistic model with context and X main effects but no interaction, scoring held-out observed targets with Brier loss. Count how many of the five survive and report all selected cases, including failures. Selectivity, catalog size, and self-selected ratings limit any conclusion about other applications.

## What happened on the invented policies

On the novel assignments with one calibration pair, partner accuracy was **61.95%** for the learned write direction, **50.01%** for the separately trained fixed direction and **36.86%** when only the next write borrowed the gate from the opposite history. Clamping the trained writer to one half gave 49.52%. Changing the gate for the trigger alone cost 25.09 points (paired seed interval +17.35 to +32.45 in favor of the correct gate). With no calibration all three interventions on the same writer produced identical predictions. The deployable value matrix survived cold reloading from just the static token reader and updated weights in all 20 exported cases. The witness states and the gating network were absent from that read. [Paired estimates and reload checks](conditional-summary.json) · [Raw results](conditional-trials.json) · [Exported weights](conditional-weights.npz)

The intended sign reversal only partly materialized. After calibrating on coordinated queues, a positive versus a negative trigger moved its partner's probability up 20.19 points (+15.42 to +24.37). In handoff incidents it moved the partner by **−0.55 points** (−4.83 to +3.80). A model can improve substantially by suppressing the wrong update while still failing to learn a reliable opposite update. After a false trigger report, a truthful subsequent check repaired 28.65% of jointly missed partners versus 23.23% with fixed writes. A false check after a true report harmed 28.93% versus 24.63% of jointly correct partners. The +4.29-point excess harm (+2.83 to +5.71) misses the bound. The false-check result conditions on the verifier actually being wrong. Averaging over 90% correct and 10% wrong returns instead, the learned update repaired 4.35 points more after a false initial report (+2.98 to +5.75) and damaged 3.87 points fewer after a true initial report (−4.65 to −3.03). A system can improve in that mixture while becoming more sensitive to a known-false check. The prespecified sign-reversal test still failed.

A simple empirical observer without audit class IDs scored **80.06%** on the same new mappings, and one given the structural rule reached 84.69%. A second calibration pair exposed the empirical observer's brittleness. Additional truthful labels drove it down to 14.52% on the unpracticed parity while the structural observer stayed at 81.93%. The historical full-map prior assigns little weight to any novel mapping. More observed labels can identify the wrong familiar map with high confidence when the missing pattern is systematic. The neural learner scored 60.76% with two calibration pairs. We fixed the sign test before training; novelty here means a new queue assignment using familiar intents. The initial logit in this experiment is zero. The reported untouched-request metric applies only when its pair was not used for calibration.

## The natural reversal screen

**None of the five selected reversals confirmed** in either the 4,991-person test split or the separately released file of 23,494 eligible raters. The closest result in the first test split had oriented effects of +7.79 and +7.07 points and a Holm-adjusted p-value of .055. In the second file the same candidate gave +4.52 and −0.63 points in its nominated directions. All five adjusted p-values there were 1.0. The saturated context-by-rating predictor had a higher Brier loss than the additive control on all five second-file cases. This weighs against using these two calibration ratings to demonstrate conditional sign reversal in Jester. It does not measure all possible representations of taste.
