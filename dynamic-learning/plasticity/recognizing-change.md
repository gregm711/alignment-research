# Can a learner recognize when its old update rule stops helping?

**Finding.** The learner could detect when its history was misleading. Revising the effects of earlier observations gave a small causal improvement. The capability target was a three-point gain over a tuned conventional learner on genuinely new functions. Neither experiment met it.

In the first eight lifetimes we followed two candidate adapters, scored their predictions before each verified observation, and used their relative performance to choose the next update direction. The learner saw neither the family's identity nor the rule specification. On new conditional tools built from previously irrelevant features, the average weight it gave the practiced direction fell from 50% initially to 33%, 17%, and 5% after 4, 8, and 16 examples. For the same operation on familiar features it was still 47% after 16. Applying another task's evidence reduced accuracy. By the time the learner recognized the mismatch, it had already written several poor updates.

The next eight lifetimes tested whether that early cost could be recovered. Three small adapters followed practiced, uniform, or formerly downweighted features. Both experimental branches saw the same labels and tracked exactly the same predictive losses. One used those losses to change its next gradient. The other recombined the already-updated weight vectors before predicting. In the direct intervention their rates and evidence temperatures were identical. The emitted 12-weight readout can run after removing the shadow learners. Continuing to adapt with this method needs all three 12-weight shadow states and their three scores.

### The task split needed a behavioral audit

Two programs written with different feature triples can compute exactly the same function because some features are products of others. Across 1,280 disjoint-feature conditional draws in the second run, 317 matched a function accessible in training or tuning. The raw protocol held out triples. We subsequently enumerated the answers on all 64 possible inputs and excluded **every conditional rule equivalent to any signed majority of three distinct features from the entire 12-feature vocabulary**. We then gave each remaining distinct function equal weight within its lifetime. This left 13–45 distinct functions per second-run lifetime. It is a post-hoc sensitivity analysis. The registered metrics and trial data are preserved. [Exhaustive audit and counts](change-task-audit.json)

The capability measure is mean held-out accuracy after 4, 8, and 16 verified examples. Intervals bootstrap eight independent lifetimes. Ridge regression, which fits ordinary output weights from the same demonstrations, won development selection in all eight second-run lifetimes. Under the original sampling it also beat the adaptive rule on every one of the five test groups, including mixed-feature conditional tools. The practical comparison independently tunes each method. The matched causal comparison isolates the benefit of changing what earlier examples contributed.

### Truthful and misleading feedback

Every test forks the same learner weights and measures predictions on a different input that is equivalent for the tool. For each source we test both a true and a false label. We then weight the counterfactuals by each source's share of true or false advice under the declared environment. Both sources are equally common. Source reliability comes from independently checked historical answers. A source-blind learner receives the same mean update strength.

On the behaviorally distinct conditional functions from the second run, using source history reduced harmful flips by **2.67 points** (95% interval −5.07 to −0.31) and changed truthful repairs by **+4.93 points** (−0.38 to +12.67). If the sources exchanged reliability after calibration, the same policy **increased harmful flips by 7.07 points** (+4.58 to +9.77). The repair difference under reversal was −1.13 points (−5.59 to +5.00). The point estimates satisfy the prespecified stable-source feedback thresholds. The interval for harm reduction includes effects smaller than the two-point practical target. The reversal is a counterfactual reweighting of actual per-source interventions, not a stream where the learner could already have observed a change. This is evidence about trusting training signals in a binary task. It leaves open how a language model responds to a person.

**Interpretation.** A learner that changes which connections it updates can identify that past specialization has become unhelpful. Accumulating an alternative set of weights lets it undo part of the damage. Ordinary ridge regression remains a strong reference because these experiments freeze the transformer's feature extractor. A stronger next test would let experience change the representation itself, use a second executable task domain, and give the model occasional fresh opportunities to verify a source after its reliability changes. Both studies used one CPU thread. Neither trained a foundation model or incurred GPU costs.

## Protocol recorded before running

Start eight fresh 100-tool lifetimes (seeds 241–248) using the frozen transformer and learned diagonal update rule from the preceding study. At a known task boundary clear the task weights. The learner receives verified demonstrations and gets no signal indicating whether the family or operation changed. Cross two factors. The tool can use the six practiced or the six previously irrelevant features. Its rule can be the practiced majority vote or a new conditional operation that selects between two switches according to a third. Hold entire feature triples out of training and development. No conditional tools enter training or tuning.

**Intervention.** Run two shadow adapters on the past demonstrations, one with the practiced update direction, one with ordinary SGD. Use their cumulative *pre-update* predictive losses to decide how much of the practiced direction to use in the next weight update of a third adapter. Save its weights at 4, 8 and 16 demonstrations. A cold read using only those weights must reproduce the predictions. Uniform, fixed practiced and tuned constant blends are controls. Include Adam and a version using the evidence of an unrelated task. Select rates, fixed blend and controller temperature on balanced development majority tasks. Compare held-out choices after 0, 4, 8 and 16 examples. Report reference accuracy of a linear readout fitted to more examples for each condition, since the conditional operation can be harder to represent.

**Capability prediction.** With paired observations, the adaptive rule will outperform the best development-selected conventional or fixed rule by three points on new conditional tools using previously irrelevant features, with a paired 95% lifetime-bootstrap interval above zero. At most two points of accuracy loss on held-out familiar tools are acceptable. Score the mean over 4, 8 and 16 verified examples. Measure recognition separately using the fraction of weight still given to the old update rule; for matched sample counts it should be higher on practiced than disjoint features. Compare to shuffling the evidence across tasks. Report storage and pass costs, and a negative outcome if detection works without improving future learning.

**Feedback prediction.** In each lifetime simulate two advisors with hidden identities and respective accuracy 90% and 55%. Estimate each reliability from 40 earlier independently checked answers per advisor. After 16 executions, ask for advice on a fresh input and measure propagation to a distinct input with the same three underlying features. Fork the same checkpoint into truthful and false advice from each advisor. Include no advice, ordinary unweighted advice, a common reliability weight, and swapped source records. Use the estimated reliability to scale the advice gradient by max(0, 2p−1). No source sees hidden answers at test time. Score the additional errors and repairs relative to the no-update copy; compare *conditional on misleading versus truthful advice* under the declared mixture of sources. Advance only if truthful-repair rate falls by at most two points and harmful flips drop by at least two relative to a source-blind, equally scaled update. Report per-source effects and eligible counts. This tests calibrated response to feedback on executable rules. It makes no assumption that a toy system's results transfer to human-facing alignment.

## Follow-up registered after observing recognition without recovery

On the new conditional operation and disjoint features, the controller assigned the practiced updates 5.1% weight after 16 examples. Its capability score remained 3.53 points below the selected baseline. Test whether revising earlier updates after more evidence arrives makes a difference. Track three independently evolving weight vectors, with directions given by the practiced gate, flat SGD, and the reciprocal of the practiced gate. Normalize each diagonal to root-mean-square one. The third branch tests a *specific* hypothesis that formerly irrelevant features may now matter. Add a mixed-feature conditional test to expose excessive specialization to a perfectly complementary family. Fit no hyperparameters on conditional or mixed tasks.

Compare two uses of the exact same branch losses and posterior weights: apply the mixture only to future gradients, or immediately use the mixture of the three fully updated weight vectors. The latter can be exported as a single adapter after each prefix. A static equal-weight ensemble, individual branch updates, a tuned uniform SGD and ridge regression on the same examples are controls. Include a version that borrows another task's mixture weights. Use eight new lifetimes (341–348), select rates and evidence temperature on majority development tasks, and report paired accuracy averaged at 4, 8, and 16 examples plus all budget curves. Match update scale between the prospective and retroactive arms by using the prospective controller's development-selected rate and temperature for both in the direct causal contrast. Also tune the retroactive arm separately for the practical comparison.

Primary prediction: rewriting previously accumulated weights beats prospective-only updating in the disjoint conditional group with a paired seed interval above zero. The capability bar remains a three-point advantage over the development-selected practical comparator on that group with no more than two points lost on familiar majority tools. Report comparisons to ridge separately regardless of which comparator wins on development. For the new learner repeat the independently checked source reliability experiment and also calculate the same feedback outcomes when the two advisors exchange reliability after calibration. No source-identity change is announced. Reliability drift can invalidate apparent alignment improvements.
