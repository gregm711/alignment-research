# Inferring and composing unfamiliar transformations

## Protocol fixed before scoring the local reader

Generate 24 independent worlds (seed 776031). Two named machines each permute four input positions in a fixed order; the permutations and arbitrary symbols change by world. A repeated-symbol example restricts machine P. One four-distinct-symbol example identifies machine Q. Two possible follow-ups demonstrate P on the same new repeated-symbol input but produce different outputs. Given the follow-up, exactly one P survives. A fresh query string uses different symbols and a different grouping of positions. Ask for P's output and, separately, Q applied after P on that same query. Both depths see identical examples. The two answer strings for each comparison are the two worlds' genuine outputs, with their letter positions balanced across worlds and all four joint fork-letter patterns equally frequent. P's query output cannot simply be copied from an example. Exclude Qs that leave either possible intermediate unchanged. For each fork check by enumerating all 24 possible permutations that both answer strings remain possible after removing either P observation and that exactly one remains with both observations. This guards against multiple-choice answers revealing the needed rule by themselves.

Use the pinned frozen Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507 4-bit MLX reader and its standard chat template. Each prompt is a fresh context; pick the greater of the first-token logits for A and B. Record complete responses, per-fork accuracy, the fraction of worlds correct on both futures, balanced chance references (50% individual, 25% joint for independent guesses), elapsed time and paired world-level uncertainty. Compare to clue-only reading (retain Q's example; delete the first P observation), a simple one-demonstration pattern matcher, and exact rule enumeration. Expect full evidence to improve individual accuracy by at least 10 percentage points over the identical readout after deletion, at each depth. A strong transfer result also needs at least 75% of worlds solved on both forks at both depths. No inference-time search or external tool is available to the neural reader. This is a bounded test of immediate induction under an explicitly stated permutation family.

## Outcomes

| Fresh-answer readout | P branch 0 / 24 | P branch 1 / 24 | Both / 24 | Q after P branch 0 / 24 | Q after P branch 1 / 24 | Both / 24 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Frozen Qwen, full evidence | 12 | 13 | 6 | 12 | 12 | 6 |
| Frozen Qwen, first P example removed | 12 | 12 | 6 | 12 | 12 | 6 |
| First permutation fitting only the later demonstration; closest candidate | 17 | 11 | 6 | 17 | 11 | 6 |
| Exact enumeration of both demonstrations | 24 | 24 | 24 | 24 | 24 | 24 |

The direct readout chose B on 95 of 96 full prompts and every clue-only prompt. Correct answer letters and the four paired branch-letter combinations were exactly balanced at each depth. Giving both demonstrations increased single-stage accuracy by 2.08 points (paired 95% world bootstrap 0.00–6.25) and compositional accuracy by zero (all paired differences zero). Both registered thresholds failed. Measured elapsed time on this host was 0.84 seconds to load, 20.42 seconds for 96 full prompts and 18.76 seconds for 96 clue-only prompts. The scoring operation compares two **first-token logits**; it provides no working tokens. The strong position preference makes the zero-deliberation result a readout failure. It does not resolve what the same weights can induce with scratch space, demonstrations of a solution procedure, or search. String permutation induction under a supplied operator family also does not establish open-ended abstraction.

For each full question the exact solver finds one P of 24. Deleting either P demonstration leaves four compatible P rules and retains both proposed outputs. The exact baseline's success comes from its explicit assumption of position permutations. The one-example matcher breaks ambiguous rules by lexicographic index and ties by choosing A. Its result depends on the sampled worlds. Reproduce with the project's MLX environment using `python run.py` in this directory.
