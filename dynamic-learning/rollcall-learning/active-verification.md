# Which independent check helps with a disputed weight update?

## Protocol fixed before evaluating the new motions

Use eligible motions 289–544 (one-based) from Congresses 117 and 118. The previous forward study scored motions 33–288. Regenerate the same panels, previous historical training and 32 recent audits, with the same model seeds and settings. Keep all four reveal orders for a motion together. Give each learner four verified ballots, a fifth potentially incorrect report, and a randomly ordered menu of eight other available ballots it can verify. Reserve every member outside the prefix and entire menu for scoring, so choosing a different member never changes the target set. This retrospective setup assumes a verifier can return an accurate ballot for any available menu member. True and inverted reports share all menu selections.

Use the same fitted historical templates to choose one or three checks. The primary selector greedily maximizes the predicted reduction in the variance of the disputed ballot. For Bernoulli outcomes this is the squared posterior covariance of a candidate and the disputed ballot divided by the candidate's predictive variance. Update the template posterior with the obtained answer before choosing again. Controls use the original random menu order, the most uncertain candidate, or the greatest predicted variance reduction summed across the other panel members outside the prefix and menu. The chooser knows four confirmed values and the identities of the available menu; it never sees the disputed answer or any unopened value. All selectors face the same menu and pay for the same number of checks.

Score the disputed ballot probability itself by Brier loss against the actual ballot. Compare mixed (90% truthful, 10% reversed source) accuracy on the reserved ballots after replay into a neural weight matrix, plus conditional truthful repairs and harmful flips relative to omitting the report. Use common initially correct/wrong query sets when comparing two different selectors. For each selection policy compare fixing the report's trust from the original four ballots versus estimating it again after the new checks, while writing exactly the same checked evidence in both. Also include constant 90% trust and the direct template forecast. The distinction matters: a chosen check can predict other targets well while doing little to validate the disputed report.

Primary test at three checks: compared with random checks and the same template-informed writing method, require lower disputed-ballot Brier loss, higher mixed target accuracy, lower harmful flips and no more than two points of lost truthful repairs, using paired 95% intervals from contiguous 16-motion blocks within the two Congresses. Report the one-check effects, all selectors, and actual conditional rates. Measure unexpected truths using only the first four verified ballots: the historical template assigns the true fifth ballot less than 20% probability. The definition is fixed across selectors and both report forks. Report results and unique motion counts there; an aggregate improvement cannot establish responsiveness to surprises. Integrate both report forks exactly. These motions test independent corroboration within one decision. They do not alone establish that a relationship has persistently changed across decisions.

## First result

At three checks on 512 motions, selecting checks about the disputed ballot reduced its Brier loss from 0.0694 to 0.0605 (difference −0.0089, paired block interval −0.0127 to −0.0051). But after the credibility estimate and verified observations were written into the neural attention matrix, mixed accuracy on other ballots **fell from 89.56% to 88.75%** (−0.82 percentage points, −1.37 to −0.22). Excess harmful flips on common initially correct queries changed by −0.19 points (−0.45 to +0.07); repairs on common initially wrong queries changed by +1.35 points (−0.29 to +2.99). Thus the joint test failed. Comparisons of each method's own repair rate condition on different initial errors and answer a different question. With one targeted check, disputed-ballot Brier improved by 0.0170; the query accuracy difference was −0.34 points (−0.95 to +0.25). With the direct template reader three checks gave 91.76% accuracy for targeted selection and 91.64% for random selection. Queries, budgets and available information were matched. The Brier gain for the disputed ballot among unexpected truths did not establish better weight learning elsewhere. ## Matched practice test specified before training

A learner practiced on random examples may not know how to write correlated, intentionally chosen evidence. Test a training-by-testing interaction prospectively on the last 256 eligible motions of each of Congresses 117 and 118, disjoint from both earlier forward studies. Train two copies from identical initialization. Both receive identical historical motion draws, first four verified ballots, disputed reports, eight-member menus, scored queries and simulated source errors. One practices with checks chosen at random and the other with checks chosen for their information about the disputed ballot. Both optimize the same end-to-end objective after one and three checks, including the same candidate-state auxiliary loss. The soft fifth label always uses the template odds conditional on the checks that arm saw. Neither sees the fifth target's truth during acquisition or inference. Both may use true outcomes on other members during practice. Use 192 batches of 24 motions from the final two thirds of the old training period (first third is the common selection archive), then 32 batches from the 32 new audits using the full old training archive for selection. Retain the prior, seeds, step sizes and capacity from the preceding model. At evaluation each copy uses either random or targeted verification, with the common historical-plus-recent archive. All data and models stay local.

Primary contrast: practice-by-selection interaction in mixed target accuracy at three checks. Require a positive lower paired 95% 16-motion block bound and a three-point advantage for targeted practice over random practice on the targeted check stream to conclude a meaningful capability advance. Report both main effects, all four cells, mixed Brier, truthful repairs and false harm with common eligibility, the direct template control and the fixed training budget. To support a joint gain require upper excess-harm bound below two points and lower truthful-repair bound above minus two. Report prefix-defined unusual truths and sample counts. Store per-motion sufficient statistics and reload at least one fast matrix independently of its support.

## Matched practice result

Three targeted checks gave 92.41% accuracy after random practice and 92.54% after targeted practice. With random checks these same learners scored 92.25% and 92.35%. The extra advantage from matching practice to test selection was **0.025 percentage points** (paired 95% block interval −0.053 to +0.101). The effect of targeted training on the targeted stream was +0.126 points (+0.049 to +0.208), well below the specified three points. Averaging over test selectors, targeted practice improved accuracy by 0.114 points (+0.043 to +0.190) and Brier loss by 0.00108. Averaging over training arms, targeted selection changed accuracy by +0.182 points (−0.108 to +0.484). The direct template forecast scored 93.33% on targeted checks and 93.16% on random checks. The small training benefit gives little reason to favor this architecture here.

On the targeted stream, common-eligibility truthful repairs changed by +0.051 points (−0.289 to +0.401) and harm from false reports by −0.029 points (−0.081 to +0.025). Both feedback tolerances passed. Genuine unexpected reports occurred in 44 reveal orders on 39 distinct motions and 53 orders on 47 motions respectively. In that subgroup the targeted-training accuracy difference was +0.354 points (−0.186 to +0.975). Only one initialization was used per Congress. Motion-block intervals describe variation across the evaluated decisions. Both final matrices for the archived example reproduced every saved probability when loaded in a fresh process using only the weights, member identities and starting bias. The two training arms here used a different shared objective and test period from the first study, so cross-study score differences cannot identify which change mattered.
