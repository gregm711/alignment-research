# What should a model learn from its next hundred experiences?

Research assessment, 24 November 2026. This is a selective reading of primary studies and released model cards. The ranking is a forecast for 2036. Published findings and proposed experiments are identified separately.

## My bet

The most promising architecture combines a capable pretrained model, a record of particular experiences, a small part of the model that experience can change, and a way to choose the next observation. Train the entire system over sequences of related tasks. Reward it when yesterday's experience reduces the number of examples it needs tomorrow, including when an old rule changes. I have more confidence in these functions than in any particular implementation. In some domains the best durable representation of a skill may be a short executable program.

Imagine teaching an agent a new spreadsheet import tool. One invoice dated 04/05 posts to the wrong month. The agent could save the correction, reverse all dates, or try importing 23/05. That last action cheaply distinguishes two competing explanations. It still needs to discover whether the convention belongs to this supplier, this import template, or every invoice. A good learner can answer that question and leave other suppliers' records alone. Storing more tokens, taking more reasoning steps, and writing weights address different pieces of this problem.

## What human efficiency means

| Comparison | Evidence and implication |
|---|---|
| How much **new experience** buys a skill? | A person who sees an unfamiliar handwritten character already knows about strokes. A model that inferred short drawing programs from reusable pieces reproduced several human one-example generalization abilities. The representational assumption does much of the work. Test transfer to an unfamiliar alphabet separately from learning the original pieces. [Lake, Salakhutdinov and Tenenbaum](https://collaborate.princeton.edu/en/publications/human-level-concept-learning-through-probabilistic-program-induct/) |
| What information arrives in one example? | A model trained on 61 hours of aligned speech and video from one child's perspective learned many word–object associations. A later study across three children and over 500 hours found transfer across children and image domains. The endpoint was grounded word learning. A child's word count misses the scene in which each word was heard. [Original study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38300999/) · [Cross-child study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42808069/) |
| Who chooses the examples? | In experiments with preschoolers, time spent exploring tracked how well observations could distinguish competing explanations. The evidence supports a role for informative exploration. For machines, DeepMind's AdA learned to adapt on a human timescale to held-out tasks in a particular 3D world after extensive meta-training. Count both the cost of acquiring the prior and the interactions needed to use it. [Children's exploration](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34127657/) · [AdA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.07608) |
| What persists after an interruption? | Complementary learning systems theory explains why it may help to store specific experiences rapidly and interleave them when changing shared knowledge. Human experiments detected replay organized by inferred relationships during awake rest. They motivate an engineering comparison with selective rehearsal. The biological implementation remains an open question. [Complementary learning systems](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7624455/) · [Human replay experiment](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31280961/) |
| What about energy? | Neural signaling, communication and keeping cells alive all consume energy. Digital systems also incur costs moving weights and state. A meaningful ratio needs the same task, success criterion, learning history and system boundary. A person's metabolic power and a cluster's total pretraining electricity answer different questions. [Brain signaling energy budget](https://doi.org/10.1097/00004647-200110000-00001) · [Cortical communication cost model](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8106317/) |

One contemporary counterexample matters. On semi-private ARC-AGI-3, GPT-6.2 Astra's best reported scores were 70.3% with the standard interface and 100% with a provider interface that carries reasoning state between requests. The peaks occurred at different reasoning settings. ARC measures action efficiency on particular interactive games. Pretraining, internal computation and transfer to an unrelated game next week are separate measurements. [ARC Prize's evaluation and configurations](https://arcprize.org/results/openai-gpt-6-2-astra)

## Architecture bets, in order

**1. Learn which experiences deserve a lasting update, and how widely it should apply.** Give the learner an episodic record it can retrieve verbatim, plus a small writable part whose changes can generalize. Optimize the decision to write against future performance. This makes a useful distinction possible: remembering the invoice is cheap, while changing a date parser can improve thousands of future imports or silently corrupt them. Delaying a change until a diagnostic example arrives has measurable value.

Three results constrain this bet. TTT-E2E used ordinary sliding-window transformers trained to update their weights on incoming context. At 3B parameters the authors reported long-context loss scaling comparable to full attention and 2.7× faster inference at 128k. Doc-to-LoRA trained a separate network to turn an unseen document into a small adapter in one pass. Both establish workable write mechanisms. In six-domain Continual Learning Bench, simply carrying examples in context beat the dedicated memory systems tested. Any new architecture must earn its advantage over retaining the evidence. [TTT-E2E](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.23675) · [Doc-to-LoRA](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v306/charakorn26a.html) · [Continual Learning Bench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.05661)

**2. Learn action-conditioned world models and how to test competing explanations.** Predict what each plausible rule says would happen if the agent took a particular action. Prefer cheap actions whose possible outcomes change the decision. Dreamer used a learned model to practice behavior in imagined futures across more than 150 tasks with a shared algorithm configuration. Separate physical-planning research released models that predict representations of future observations from proposed actions. Planning to reach a known goal and choosing an action to learn an unknown rule deserve separate scores. I expect the latter to be particularly valuable in software, where many experiments are cheap and reversible. [Dreamer](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-025-08744-2) · [JEPA physical planning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.24497)

**3. Spend recurrent computation and replay where each changes future performance.** Reusing a block trades execution time for parameter reuse. In a preregistered, data-limited text experiment, a 12-layer stack run four times beat a 48-layer stack by 5.4 points on grammatical judgments at matched inference compute. Elsewhere in BabyLM, an ordinary autoregressive transformer led a 432-run comparison across the token mixers and objectives tested. Rehearsal also has an opportunity cost. In continual pretraining across languages, a small amount of replay was valuable, but increasing model size was more compute-efficient than high replay rates. I would use loops for difficult inference and test replay for durable transfer independently. [Recurrent-depth study](https://aclanthology.org/2026.babylm-main.43/) · [BabyLM controls](https://aclanthology.org/2026.babylm-main.7/) · [Replay at scale](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v330/abbes26a.html)

**4. Let a frontier model design the smaller learner's update rule.** A frontier model has already written a tabular predictor from zero weights that scored 0.849 mean AUC across 20 datasets. A pretrained baseline reached 0.876. Here the writer encoded a statistical procedure that could use new examples. The bigger bet is that a writer can discover a better procedure for a *changing* world. Charge for the writer's search, disconnect it, and compare its result to the best ordinary program and trained learner at the same memory and interaction budget. [Weights & Caches](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.20174v1) · [Local weight-writing review](weight-space-programming.md)

## The experiment that could change the ranking

Give every learner the same unfamiliar tool family, the same evidence budget, and a stream of changes. Some are one-off exceptions, some affect one tool, and some apply to a whole family. Randomize these causes and withhold diagnostic examples unless the learner requests one. After a context reset, score examples needed to reach a fixed accuracy, mistakes on unaffected tasks, transfer to a second family, memory bytes, and total compute. Separate the value of **choosing the next observation** from the value of **retaining it** by replaying identical observations to a second learner. Compare full context, searchable episodes, executable rule induction, ordinary adapter training, and learned selective updates. Give all methods the same feedback about which executions succeeded.

The local plasticity pilot illustrates why the second family matters. After clearing the old adapter, the experienced rule scored 65.0% versus 64.3% for the tuned conventional control on related tools. On the disjoint family it scored 56.7% versus 64.4%. A mechanism that makes yesterday's regularities easier to learn can make tomorrow's exceptions harder. Continual-ARC offers a public, complementary test with recurring tasks, dormant gaps, priced feedback, and separate evaluation on unseen tasks. Its authors found a simple example cache surprisingly competitive and transfer from weight-based imitation of a stronger model. The latter includes teacher supervision. [Local pilot and follow-up](plasticity/README.md) · [Saved follow-up results](plasticity/followup-summary.json) · [Continual-ARC](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14505)

I would move active experimentation to first place if choosing better observations produced most of the gain and storing raw observations matched learned updates. I would raise the weight-writing bet if a disconnected writer's learner transferred to an independently designed second task family and beat a comparably budgeted program. Either result would teach us more about human-like efficiency than another improvement in immediate recall.
