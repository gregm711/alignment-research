# Independent review of the Inkling experiments

**Verdict.** The final-pass message transplant reproduces exactly on the released model. Extra passes improve multi-step answer accuracy on both public prompt sets under two scoring rules. The reported arithmetic robustness reverses when the number is scored. The lens results show that a known intermediate word can often be found somewhere in the network. Identifying the model’s intermediate on a new prompt, without knowing the answer or the layer in advance, remains an open problem.

Reviewed repository revision `71098e5`, including the public lens evaluation added during review. I inspected all seven experiment scripts, both paper fetchers, the research notes, the saved outputs and their provenance. I ran two A100 checks on the released checkpoint, recounted item-level results and checked the pinned model forward pass. The companion checkpoint, lens fitting, direction edits, request generation and WikiText sweep received code and artifact review only. The two fresh runs and their per-item outputs are available as [review evidence](../jlens-inkling/results/independent-review.json).

## 1. Arithmetic robustness fails at the answer position

`noise.py` compares the top token on a perturbed prompt with the clean top token. Its arithmetic prompts end at the equals sign. On all 12, I reproduced the entire original retention table and confirmed that **every clean prediction was a space**. The corrected pass sweep had already accounted for this tokenization. The noise experiment did not.

I kept the original checkpoint, probe order, noise seeds, hooks and all ten settings. I ran each condition again with the space appended, as in the corrected sweep, and compared the decoded token with the gold number. All 12 clean answers were correct. The prompt gains a position, so this measures a fresh intervention at the answer-producing step. The rows give counts out of 12.

| Perturbation | Space retained, α=2 | Number correct, α=2 | Space retained, α=16 | Number correct, α=16 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Restart, last position | 12 | 12 | 12 | 12 |
| Restart, every position | 12 | 12 | 11 | 0 |
| Messages into pass 2 | 12 | 12 | 12 | 0 |
| Messages into pass 3 | 12 | **0** | 7 | 1 |
| Messages into pass 3, last position | 12 | 11 | 12 | 4 |

Withdraw the general claim of arithmetic immunity and the mechanism proposed to explain it. The answer survives several specific interventions. For the factual tiers, the published statistic likewise measures agreement with the clean top token. Ground-truth accuracy and token-boundary checks are needed to interpret those columns as facts retained. ## 2. The causal message-swap result reproduces

I independently implemented capture and replacement of the 24 message-builder outputs, checked full-vocabulary logits on self-swaps and recorded every ordered capital pair. All eight self-swaps reproduced the logits exactly. Seven prompts had the correct clean language. On their 42 ordered pairs, replacing messages into pass 2 produced the donor language 8 times. Replacing messages into pass 3 produced it **33 times**. All seven recipients showed transfers, ranging from three to six of six donors. Adding pass 2 to the full pass-3 replacement changed no logit on any pair. In the upstream forward pass, the final pass restarts from the pre-loop state and receives the replaced messages, which explains that equality for full-position replacements. [Model implementation](https://github.com/huggingface/transformers/blob/046bdb8cc2cee29d233964bb77fe6fd37cfe4aa3/src/transformers/models/inkling2_loop/modeling_inkling2_loop.py#L1397-L1427)

The observed effect is causal on this template. Its interpretation is underdetermined. City, country and language all change together. The experiment cannot tell which of them travels through the channel. Forty-two pairs reuse seven prompts, so treating them as 42 independent questions would overstate precision. The original last-position-only swap produced zero donor answers, although two outcomes changed to other words in pass 3. That localizes the intervention only to the extent that replacing the final position alone was insufficient.

On the sibling, the saved pass-3 count is 17/42. Within-operation arithmetic swaps retained the original answer in 120/120 cases on each checkpoint. Unlike the noise test, the swap script handles the numerical answer position. The released model produced the recipient’s numbers combined with the donor’s operation in 13/112 **pooled bidirectional** pairs. The net count alone cannot attribute all 13 to multiplication donors. These findings motivate testing whether reusable intermediate information crosses the boundary. [Original swaps](../jlens-inkling/results/message-swap.json) · [Pair construction](../jlens-inkling/patch.py)

## 3. The public scorer changes the levels, but the pass effect survives

I joined saved top-token strings to the upstream gold labels by item name. As a sensitivity check, I stripped whitespace, folded case and required the entire decoded token to equal the target or a named intermediate. The original score assumes a leading space for many candidates, even after an opening quotation mark. It marks some literal answers such as `up`, `January` and `birth` wrong. It also credits the prefix ` begin` for `beginnings`. This reanalysis uses the same generated tokens and the same included items. It still misses correct multi-token continuations, and the original prompt construction uses the gold target to decide whether to supply a space.

| Checkpoint and public set | Original target hits, passes 1 → 3 | Whole decoded token, passes 1 → 3 |
|---|---:|---:|
| Released, multi-hop | 13 → 32 / 80 | **16 → 40 / 80** |
| Variable-loop, multi-hop | 32 → 36 / 80 | 39 → 45 / 80 |
| Released, order of operations | 4 → 35 / 55 | **3 → 35 / 55** |
| Variable-loop, order of operations | 33 → 39 / 55 | 33 → 39 / 55 |

The corrected released-model multi-hop gain comprises 25 items gained and one lost. Its one-pass output visibly names a listed intermediate **5/80**, compared with the reported 2/80, and 1/80 at three passes. Early exit rarely reveals the bridge under either rule. The sibling gains less on these items. Both the fixed checkpoint’s off-training-depth exits and the output scorer limit claims about precisely when computation occurs. [Item-level outputs](../jlens-inkling/results/early-exit-public.json) · [Upstream prompts](https://github.com/anthropics/jacobian-lens/tree/581d398613e5602a5af361e1c34d3a92ea82ba8e/data/evaluations)

The 44 hand-written rows reproduce the reported pass counts, including 3, 11 and 15 correct two-hop answers out of 16 on the released model. The ordinary-text aggregates cover 6,660 tokens from the first 60 eligible paragraphs. Released-model one-pass agreement with pass 3 is 56.8%, while only 51.8% retain that same prediction at *every* intervening pass. The sibling figures are 86.2% and 83.1%. These are different definitions of “decided.” TwoHopFact yielded 332 scorable items and only four released-model items with both individual hops scored correct (five for the sibling). The planned public comparison of composition versus recall remains unresolved. ## 4. The lens detects some gold words. Its benchmark label needs correction

Anthropic defines multi-hop pass@k by scoring each listed intermediate across layers, then averaging the fraction recovered within each item. The repository takes the single best rank across **all** listed intermediates and spellings. Nine of its 88 included items have multiple intermediates. The saved minimum discards the individual ranks required to recover the upstream metric exactly. Also, taking the best of 24, 36 or 48 source layers gives the deeper runs more opportunities to score. The saved rows omit the winning layer. [Upstream definition](https://github.com/anthropics/jacobian-lens/blob/581d398613e5602a5af361e1c34d3a92ea82ba8e/data/evaluations/README.md#lens-eval-multihop) · [Evaluation code](../jlens-inkling/lenseval.py)

I reproduced every cell of the new table from its item rows. With three passes, the plain logit lens places a listed word in the top ten somewhere on **58/88** items, versus **9/88** for the next-item control. The Jacobian lens gives 52/88 versus 4/88. Only 75 items overlap the early-exit cohort of 80. On those same 75 at one pass, the plain lens gives 24 top-one hits, the output visibly gives five, and **20 have a plain-lens hit without an output hit**. Thus the qualitative observation survives matching items and correcting the output score. The lens strips trailing whitespace and the output scorer selectively restores it, so even this paired comparison can use different input tokens. A next-item control also depends on dataset ordering and token frequency. For a usable monitor, select the layer and decoding rule in advance, use matched inputs and score on held-out prompt families. [Lens rows](../jlens-inkling/results/lens-eval-multihop.json)

The earlier eight-probe lens medians also reproduce. For the first pass, targeting its own output improves the median best intermediate rank from 6,924 to 133.5. On the last pass, the plain lens reaches median rank 12 against the Jacobian lens’s 25. Each Jacobian lens was fitted on three paragraphs. The first-look text file used an older scorer, as the README discloses. I checked pass indexing against the pinned model and exercised the actual adapter on a small repeated PyTorch module. All 49 hooks fired once in order on two consecutive forwards. The checkpoint’s post-loop layers run even at one pass, so an early-exit output includes their decoding work. [Lens fit](../jlens-inkling/fit.py) · [Saved ranks](../jlens-inkling/results/lens-per-pass.json)

## 5. Direction edits and refusal measurements support narrower claims

At fourfold edit strength the direction experiment records 14 donor-language hits from editing pass 3 alone and seven from editing all passes. At ordinary strength the target count is zero everywhere. Its unrelated-direction control records only “stays” and “other.” A coincidental target would be counted as “other,” and the control was run only for the all-pass condition. The reported 19/42 changed control answers quantify disruption but cannot establish the target-hit baseline for pass 3. In addition, the update exchanges the two scalar projections exactly only for orthogonal unit directions (apart from degenerate cases). For directions with dot product c, a strength-one edit leaves a fraction c of the original projection difference in each updated coordinate relative to an exact exchange. The script normalizes but does not orthogonalize or report their correlations. The targeted intervention can work late on this template. Its data leave the proposed mechanism of earlier edits being recomputed open. [Direction edit](../jlens-inkling/clamp.py) · [Counts](../jlens-inkling/results/direction-swap.json)

The noise study’s refusal measure detects phrases in ten tokens from a prefilled response. Its “benign intact” count includes every detected refusal, and 13/24 clean benign requests already trigger that detector. At α=2 on all pass-3 messages, the figures are 0/24 harmful refusal flags and 3/24 benign replies meeting that proxy. These observations show co-occurring changes in two proxies. They cannot determine harmful compliance or establish the order in which safety and competence fail. The message perturbation also touches four vectors at each of twelve layers, whereas the restart intervention touches one layer. Equal per-vector norm ratios give different total interventions. “Most fragile channel” requires a stated perturbation budget. [Definitions and settings](../jlens-inkling/noise.py)

## 6. Prior art, library and reproducibility

- Goodfire studied this exact model’s cross-loop communication and compared logit, tuned and coda lenses on release day. They explicitly left Jacobian-lens work open. Thinking Machines reported that the variable-loop model can follow longer fictional lookup chains with more passes. Those studies materially narrow the novelty claim. The project’s clearest contribution is its task-specific causal intervention and its comparison with lexical readouts. [Goodfire](https://www.goodfire.com/research/interpreting-inkling-2-mini/) · [Thinking Machines](https://thinkingmachines.ai/news/inkling-2-mini/) · [Earlier work on other looped models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.01924)
- All 160 indexed local document paths resolve on this checkout and the indexed PDFs have PDF headers. The two documented fetch commands cannot regenerate the last 21-entry section. The first fetcher overwrites the index, the second appends, and running the second twice duplicates sections. A partial download can leave a partial index while the script reports problems only in its output. Use a temporary output directory for rebuilds until the index is reproducible. [Index](../papers/README.md) · [First fetcher](../papers/fetch.py) · [Lab fetcher](../papers/fetch_labs.py)
- The state-of-the-art note discloses a selective reading of abstracts and openings. Treat it as a reading map. The Hugging Face keyword snapshot omits query parameters and model IDs, and download counts cannot support its conclusion about millions of individual users. Benchmark the opportunity to audit modified models on evidence about evaluation coverage. A keyword search alone cannot establish that coverage. [Reading scope](sota-2026.md) · [Landscape](huggingface-landscape.md) · [Proposed research directions](directions.md)
- The later scripts pin Transformers. Model and dataset revisions and the lens dependency are generally floating. The local fit logs identify lens revision `581d398` and show the initial Transformers branch differed from the later pin. Record revisions and item IDs with future outputs. Several experiments retain aggregate counts and only the first few example pairs. A rerun can overwrite the default result filenames. The spend ledger contains estimates. These checks covered selected primary sources and the full index structure, without a paper-by-paper validation of the library’s scientific claims.

## Publication decision

I would publish a short note on the replicated transfer, the corrected pass sweep and the paired lens result. Put the arithmetic correction beside the original table and qualify the refusal and semantic claims. I would prioritize one discriminating experiment next. Use fresh two-step lookup tables where the donor answer, recipient answer and recipient’s second lookup applied to the donor’s intermediate are three distinct values. Align token positions, verify both clean answers, specify the predicted third answer before the swap and report every eligible pair by task family. A reproducible shift to that third value would provide evidence for a reusable intermediate that the current capital experiment cannot identify.
